# Causes of global warming

Farming has caused about 18% of the warming over 2015–2024. Together with land use, it has caused about 31%. Fossil fuels and industry cause the most warming. Air pollution cools the planet, which masks part of the warming.

## What the chart shows

*Causes of warming* is a tab of the Explore charts. It shows how much of the warming since 1850 comes from each cause:

- fossil fuels and industry;
- farming;
- land use: its CO₂, and its albedo change, which cools;
- waste: landfills and wastewater;
- air pollution, which cools;
- the natural factors: the sun and volcanoes;
- the other human emissions.

After today, each cause's band shows what is left of it once the levers you chose act on it. The band without the levers is drawn as a dashed outline. The causes of past warming are the ones that keep causing it, and each lever reduces one cause or more (see [Levers](#method-levers)).

A switch above the chart, *All gases*, *CO₂*, *Methane*, *N₂O* and *HFCs*, shows one greenhouse gas at a time. For that gas, it shows how much warming each cause makes, with and without the levers (see "Each gas by cause" below). Air pollution and the natural factors are not greenhouse gases, so they appear under *All gases* only.

## Attribution, not correlation

Emissions and global temperature have both risen since 1850. So has almost everything else people do: population, energy use, cement, cars. Two rising lines say nothing about how much one causes the other.

Answering it needs a model of how the climate responds to emissions. How much cooler would the world be today if one cause had never emitted anything, everything else being the same? That is an attribution, and it is what the chart shows.

The model is FaIR, the climate model described on the [temperature page](#method-climate). It is run once with all emissions, and again with one cause's emissions taken out. A cause's contribution in a year is the difference between the two temperatures.

### Farming's share

**Agriculture has caused about 0.21 °C of the 1.19 °C of warming over 2015–2024, about 18%.** With land use, which is mostly clearing land for farming, the two add up to about 0.37 °C, or 31%. Land use's CO₂ warms by 0.25 °C, and its albedo change cools by 0.09 °C.

Up to 2050, agriculture's share stays near 15% on SSP2-4.5, and its warming grows to 0.29 °C. On SSP1-2.6, agricultural methane falls by a third by 2050, and agriculture's warming reaches 0.27 °C. On SSP3-7.0, agricultural methane grows by 45%, but the effect has not had time to show yet: agriculture's warming reaches 0.28 °C.

These figures count only warming since 1850–1900. Farming was already emitting methane and N₂O (nitrous oxide) before then. Compared with a world without it, farming had warmed the world by about 0.10 °C on average over 1850–1900. That warming is part of the baseline, not of the figures above.

Most of farming's warming is methane, and most of the rest is N₂O. Its CO₂ is small. Land use emits only CO₂ here, so it appears under CO₂ alone. Its albedo change is not a greenhouse gas, so it appears under *All gases* only, and CO₂'s figures leave it out.

Methane's *interactions and other minor sources* holds the warming the named sources do not cover: 0.10 °C over 2015–2024. It holds two effects of taking all of a gas out at once. First, methane's lifetime gets shorter as there is less of it, so taking all of it out cools more than taking each source's part out in turn. Second, farming was already emitting methane before 1850, and that warming is part of the 1850–1900 baseline (see "Farming's share"). It also holds the methane of the minor sources that no cause covers.

## Why air pollution cools

Air pollution's particles reflect sunlight back to space. They also make clouds brighter and longer-lived. Both cool the planet.

IPCC AR6 (WG1, Summary for Policymakers A.1.3 and Chapter 7) assesses that human drivers other than greenhouse gases, principally aerosols, have cooled the world by 0.0 to 0.8 °C. They mask about 0.5 °C of the warming the greenhouse gases cause.

In FaIR's runs here the cooling is larger: 0.67 °C (0.26–1.26) over 2015–2024. There are two reasons. Taking all the aerosols out at once counts their whole effect, including the steepest part of the cloud response. And FaIR's ensemble spans a wide range of aerosol strength.

The cooling shrinks as air quality improves, so it masks less of the warming. China's controls on sulphur from power plants in the 2010s cut it. So did the 2020 limit on the sulphur in ships' fuel (IMO 2020). The scenarios cut it further as fossil fuel use falls.

The scenarios spread the shipping cut over 2015–2020, and FaIR gives a tonne of ships' sulphur the same effect as any other tonne. The abrupt 2020 step is therefore not in these figures. Studies put it at 0.03–0.08 °C of extra warming over the 2020s (see the [temperature page](#method-climate), "Why 2023 and 2024 were so warm").

### What the levers do to air pollution

No lever in Planetshift targets air pollution. The energy, heating and industry levers still cut it, because they cut the fuel that is burnt:

- less sulphur and organic carbon, which cool;
- less black carbon, which warms;
- less NOx (nitrogen oxides), whose ozone warms and whose shortening of methane's lifetime cools;
- less carbon monoxide and VOCs (volatile organic compounds), whose ozone warms.

So when those levers act, the air-pollution band's cooling shrinks after today. That change is the warming air pollution no longer masks. The levers also cut the CO₂ emitted and the methane leaked while producing the fuel, which shrinks the fossil band.

With every energy, heating and industry lever in full on SSP2-4.5, the cooling lost about equals the gains around 2030. After that, the gains are larger. In 2050, −0.08 °C of cooling is lost. Against it, the fuel's supply, methane and N₂O give +0.12 °C, and the levers' own CO₂ gives +0.26 °C (see [Energy and transport](#method-sectors)). In the breakdown under the chart, these levers show under *air pollution* with the warming they add there.

## How each cause is computed

FaIR is run with all 841 members of its calibrated ensemble on each scenario's full emissions, from 1750 to 2100. Up to 2022, the emissions are the history FaIR's calibration 1.4.1 was constrained on. After that they are the scenario's, from RCMIP v5.1.0, joined to that history with a gap that fades out over 15 years (see the [temperature page](#method-climate)). FaIR is run again with one cause's emissions taken out. A cause's contribution in a year is the temperature with everything minus the temperature without that cause, relative to 1850–1900 like the total.

What each cause takes out:

- **Fossil fuels and industry**: FaIR's fossil and industrial CO₂, less agriculture's urea and liming CO₂, so no tonne is taken out twice. Also the CH₄ (methane) and N₂O of the IPCC categories *energy* (1) and *industrial processes* (2). Up to 2023 they come from PRIMAP-hist. After that they come from each scenario's *Fossil and Industrial* CH₄ (less its waste) and N₂O, scaled to meet PRIMAP-hist in 2023. The CO₂ of burning waste stays here.
- **Farming**: the CO₂, CH₄ and N₂O of the IPCC category *agriculture* (M.AG: livestock enteric fermentation and manure, rice, managed soils and fertilisers, residue burning, urea and liming). For 1750–2023 they come from PRIMAP-hist v2.8, world total, country-reported scenario (HISTCR). From 2024, they follow each scenario's own agricultural trajectory (RCMIP's *AFOLU|Agriculture* CH₄ and *AFOLU* N₂O), scaled so it meets PRIMAP-hist in 2023. The scenarios have no variable for CO₂ from urea and liming, so it is held at its 2023 level.
- **Land use**: all of FaIR's land-use CO₂ (deforestation and other land-use change). Taking it out leaves the surface change in place. That change is mostly albedo, because cleared land reflects more sunlight. Some of it is irrigation. FaIR would derive it from cumulative land-use CO₂, which grows as long as any land is cleared. Here it is prescribed instead, from RCMIP's albedo forcing for each SSP. That forcing is built from the scenario's land-use maps. It is −0.20 W m⁻² in 2019 (AR6's value) and then follows the land each scenario clears or lets grow back. Each FaIR member scales it by its own land-use forcing factor. *Land use: albedo (cooling)* is the full run minus a run with that forcing at zero. *Land use: CO₂* is the run without land-use CO₂. Its history up to 2022 is the calibration's, built on the Global Carbon Budget: 245 billion tonnes of carbon over 1750–2019, against 190 in RCMIP's history. Each SSP's land-use CO₂ follows from 2023. PRIMAP-hist's own LULUCF category was not used. It is built from national inventories that count the uptake of managed forests, so it turns negative in recent decades and does not measure land-use change.
- **Waste**: the CH₄ and N₂O of the IPCC category *waste* (4): landfills, wastewater and the burning of waste. Up to 2023 they come from PRIMAP-hist. After that, the CH₄ follows each scenario's *Fossil and Industrial|Waste*, scaled to meet PRIMAP-hist in 2023. The scenarios have no waste N₂O, so it is held at its 2023 level. No lever acts on landfills or sewage directly. The waste of the food the levers no longer produce counts here (see "What the levers do to each gas").
- **Air pollution (cooling)**: the aerosols and their precursors as FaIR runs them, from every source: sulphur dioxide, black and organic carbon, ammonia and nitrogen oxides. Each is brought back to its pre-industrial (1750) emissions rather than to none.
- **Natural factors (sun and volcanoes)**: FaIR run with every human emission held at its 1750 level, so only the solar and volcanic forcing change the climate, relative to its own 1850–1900 mean. The dips after Krakatoa (1883), Agung (1963), El Chichón (1982) and Pinatubo (1991) show here as natural effects, not in a human cause.
- **Other human emissions**: what is left, so that the causes add up to the total. It holds the methane of fires and the other sources not named above, the fluorinated gases and the other minor gases, the ozone precursors carbon monoxide and volatile organic compounds, and the interactions between the causes. With the levers, a change in food production avoids some of the cold chain's refrigerants, which are taken off this cause. It avoids some of the methane and N₂O of food waste and wastewater too, which are taken off *Waste*, and some of the energy after the farm, taken off *Fossil fuels and industry* (see [Emissions](#method-emissions), *After the farm*).

The median of *other human emissions* is set so that the medians add up exactly to the median total. Its 5–95% range is computed member by member.

### Why the causes do not quite add up

The effects of taking causes out one at a time do not quite add up to the effect of taking them out together. With everything else present, methane's lifetime and the CO₂ sinks respond a little differently.

Taking the four greenhouse causes (fossil fuels and industry, farming, land use, waste) out together gives 0.044 °C more warming in 2024 (0.06 °C in 2050) than taking them out one by one and adding the results. Air pollution interacts more with the others. The nitrogen oxides among it shorten methane's lifetime, and the cooler world it makes takes up more CO₂.

These interactions sit in *other human emissions*. This is why that band is larger than the minor gases alone would make it.

### How each gas is computed

The same runs are made gas by gas. A gas's warming is the temperature with everything minus the temperature with all of that gas's emissions brought back to their pre-industrial (1750) level: CO₂ from fossil fuels, industry and land use, or all methane, or all N₂O, or all eleven HFCs that FaIR runs. CO₂'s warming leaves out land use's albedo change. Taking land-use CO₂ out removes that change too. A cause's part of it is the temperature with everything minus the temperature without that cause's emissions of the gas alone, the same emissions as above. Methane has one more part, *Forest, grassland and peat fires*: RCMIP's methane from burning forests, grassland and peat above its 1750 level, about 3.6 Mt a year in 2022 out of 13. The rest is the fires of 1750. Agricultural waste burning is farming's. *Interactions and other minor sources* is the gas's warming minus the named parts, and its median is set so that the medians add up to the gas's. The scenarios give HFCs for the world only, not by cause, so all of their warming is other sources and interactions: 0.015 °C over 2015–2024 and, on SSP2-4.5, 0.07 °C in 2100.

Land use's part of CO₂ is land use's own run, as land use emits nothing else here. That makes thirteen more runs of each scenario: farming's and fossil fuels' part of each of CO₂, methane and N₂O, waste's part of methane and N₂O, the fires' methane, and each of the four gases taken out in full.

### What the levers do to each gas

After today, each cause's band in a gas is what is left once the levers have cut that cause's emissions of the gas. The levers' total change comes from FaIR run in the app, and is split between causes and gases in proportion to each one's sum of responses (see [Climate](#method-climate) and [Levers](#method-levers)). With permafrost on, each cause's share of the change in its release is split between the cause's gases in proportion to the size of each. The levers cut the methane and N₂O of the food chain's waste, which come off each gas's *Waste*, and its refrigerants, which come off the HFCs' other sources.

Some of what the energy levers change is not a greenhouse gas: aviation's non-CO₂ forcing (its contrails and NOx) and the air pollution of the fuel they no longer burn, including the methane NOx leaves in the air. These count under *All gases* only, in fossil fuels and in air pollution, so a cause's gases do not quite add up to its band under *All gases*.

## How the numbers compare with other studies

- **Livestock, 2010.** A 2018 study found direct livestock CH₄ and N₂O caused about 19% of the modelled warming in 2010. Here all of agriculture causes about 19% of the 2010–2019 warming.
- **Agricultural methane, 2010–2019.** A 2024 report used an earlier FaIR calibration (1.3.1). It put agricultural methane alone at about 0.19 °C of about 1.06 °C of warming in 2010–2019, about 40% of the 0.5 °C from all methane. Here methane, N₂O and CO₂ from agriculture together give 0.20 °C. The difference comes mostly from the calibration. v1.4.1 gives all methane emissions 0.39 °C of warming in 2010–2019, against 0.49 °C for v1.3.1 and 0.5 °C (0.3 to 0.8) in AR6's assessment. The 0.10 °C already in the 1850–1900 baseline also lowers agriculture's figure more than the other causes' figures. A test checks that agriculture's warming over 2010–2019 is between 0.12 and 0.30 °C and between 12% and 30% of the total.
- **Food, by 2100.** A 2023 study held today's diets per person and let food consumption grow with population. It found that this "could add nearly 1 °C to warming by 2100" from emissions after 2020 (0.7 to 0.9 °C in its preprint). Three quarters of it came from ruminant meat, dairy and rice, and more than 55% of it was avoidable. Run the same way (agriculture and land-use emissions taken out from 2020 only), Planetshift finds 0.31 °C (0.21–0.44) on SSP2-4.5 and 0.56 °C (0.41–0.78) on SSP3-7.0. The gap is expected, for three reasons:
  - that study counts the whole food chain, including about 5 billion tonnes a year of CO₂ from energy, processing and transport, which here sits under fossil fuels;
  - it holds diets per person fixed while population grows, whereas SSP2-4.5's agricultural methane grows by only 4% to 2050 and its land-use CO₂ falls to near zero by 2100;
  - it uses another climate model (MAGICC6).

  SSP3-7.0, the closest to the study's high-population baseline, comes nearest. A test checks the SSP2-4.5 figure lies between a quarter of its lower bound and its upper bound, and that SSP3-7.0 is higher.
- **Warming by gas, 2024.** Our World in Data shows the warming by gas and source of Jones et al. (2023, data updated in 2025). Against Planetshift's medians in 2024 on SSP2-4.5: fossil CO₂ 0.81 °C there and 0.71 °C here, agricultural methane 0.16 and 0.15 °C, agricultural N₂O 0.061 and 0.066 °C. Land-use CO₂ is 0.41 °C there and 0.25 °C here, not counting its albedo change. The history explains part of the gap: 220 billion tonnes of carbon over 1850–2023 in the calibration's, against 243 in the Global Carbon Budget 2025. Fossil methane is 0.22 °C there and 0.11 °C here. Planetshift counts 0.07 °C of methane under waste and 0.11 °C under interactions and other minor sources.

## Assumptions

- Agriculture is the IPCC category M.AG of PRIMAP-hist v2.8 up to 2023. The country-reported scenario (HISTCR) is used because the third-party scenario is no longer maintained. Land-use change is outside it. 2024 and 2025 are left to the scenarios, because PRIMAP-hist fills its latest years by extrapolation where reported data end (not checked year by year for agriculture).
- From 2024, each scenario's agricultural CH₄ and N₂O trajectory is scaled by one constant factor so that it meets PRIMAP-hist in 2023. The scenarios' N₂O variable is all of *AFOLU*, which also includes biomass burning. The scaling absorbs the difference in level.
- Agricultural CO₂ (urea and liming) is held at its 2023 level after 2023. PRIMAP-hist has none before 1990.
- Every emission up to 2022 is the history FaIR's calibration 1.4.1 was constrained on, so the runs match the inputs its parameters were fitted to. Its land-use CO₂ is GCP-based, 245 billion tonnes of carbon over 1750–2019, and not the more recent Global Carbon Budget 2025, which puts net land-use emissions at 5.0 ± 2.6 billion tonnes of CO₂ a year over 2015–2024, with gross deforestation about 7.0. Its 1750 methane and N₂O are the calibration's pre-industrial levels, which taking all of a gas out brings it back to.
- The scenarios join that history in 2023 with the gap of 2022 fading out over 15 years, rather than being scaled, so a series that crosses zero (land-use CO₂ in SSP1-2.6) keeps its sign.
- Land use's albedo change is RCMIP's albedo forcing for each SSP. It is prescribed to FaIR in place of the cumulative-CO₂ proxy, and it includes irrigation. The levers change it only through regrowth, as land that grows back darkens (see [Land](#method-land), *The albedo of the land growing back*).
- Waste's N₂O is held at its 2023 level, as the scenarios give none.
- Fossil and industrial CH₄ and N₂O are PRIMAP-hist energy and industrial processes up to 2023. After that, they are the scenario's fossil and industrial emissions (CH₄ without waste), scaled to meet them.
- Air pollution is every source's sulphur dioxide, black and organic carbon, ammonia and nitrogen oxides, farming's ammonia included, brought back to 1750 levels. Carbon monoxide and volatile organic compounds stay with *other human emissions*.
- The levers' change in air pollution is counted with FaIR's forcing per kg and the methane NOx and VOCs leave. This is the same physics, linearised at the scenario's emissions. The levers' CO and VOCs still count under air pollution, as part of what burning the fuel emits. This is a small mismatch with the band.
- The natural factors are a separate run with natural forcing alone, not a removal. *Other human emissions* is the total less every named cause and the natural factors.
- A removal never takes a species' emissions below zero, except for CO₂. The scenarios' carbon removal takes net CO₂ below zero (fossil CO₂ on SSP1-2.6 from 2079). Taking a cause out leaves the removal in place. The world without farming keeps it, and the world without fossil fuels and industry keeps only farming's CO₂.
- The parts of each gas come from the same emissions as the causes. A gas has no split by source other than the one the causes give.
- The full-emission runs are also the scenario temperatures on the [temperature page](#method-climate).
