# Levers

Every way Planetshift offers to limit warming is a lever, and none is preferred: energy and transport, buildings and industry, what people eat, farm practices, less food wasted, an end to clearing forest, and carbon stored in farmland. The **Levers** section of Explore groups them under the cause of warming each mainly acts on (see [Causes of global warming](#method-link)): fossil fuels and industry (energy and transport, buildings and industry), farming (diet, farm practices, food waste) and land use (deforestation, carbon in farmland). The causes are ordered by the warming each causes by the chart's horizon under the chosen scenario, so fossil fuels come first; within a cause, the groups are ordered by the warming each would avoid alone and in full (the figure beside it). That order is worked out again when an option, the horizon or the data change, never while a slider moves, so a group does not jump under the pointer. Each group is a fold, closed at first, with a button that adopts all its levers in full and one that resets them. Each lever is a slider from 0 to 100%, the share of it adopted; the diet's main slider is the share of animal foods moved to plant foods. Every lever ramps in from today and is reached in full in the year set at the top of the panel, *All levers fully in place by*.

A lever group can act on more than one cause. The diet changes farm emissions (farming) and, through the clearing tied to what is produced and the land it frees, deforestation and regrowth (land use); less waste does the same. Their avoided warming is split by cause by gas and source: farm methane, nitrous oxide and CO₂ count in farming; the CO₂ of forest clearing, regrowth and carbon stored in farmland count in land use; fossil CO₂ and aviation's non-CO₂ forcing count in fossil fuels and industry. The Causes of warming chart takes each cause's part off its band after today, and the summary lists the parts under the same causes, with the same names and colours.

## The lever groups

**Farm practices** (see [Farm practices](#method-practices)). Seven practices, each cutting part of one or more sources of an item's emissions: feed additives and breeding act on cattle's enteric methane, manure management on the methane and nitrous oxide of stored manure, drained paddies on rice methane, nitrogen efficiency and inhibitors on the nitrous oxide of fertilisers, clean energy on the CO₂ of fuel. For a country, item and gas, the share of the emissions removed is the sum over sources of the source's share of the gas (`breakdown.json`) times the share removed from that source; a practice removes adoption × applicable share in the country × reduction. A practice limited to some food groups (feed additives reach cattle only) acts on those only. They cut the emissions of the production with the shift, so their effect is the practice on today's production plus its effect on what the shift changes.

**Food waste** (see [Food loss and waste](#method-waste)). Two levers: waste after shops, which lowers the food supply a country needs by the lever × its waste share, after the diet shift; and losses before shops, which lower each item's loss share of supply. Less supply needs less production, so less land and fewer emissions, through the same supply chain as the shift, feed included. What people eat does not change, so the protein and calories shown are those of the diet.

**Deforestation** (see [Deforestation](#method-deforestation)). Clearing forest for farming releases about 2 Gt of CO₂ a year (`deforestation.json`, by country and item). The model holds it as a constant yearly stream and counts only its change. The clearing tied to an item moves with the item's production in the country where it is cleared, so a shift away from beef lowers pasture clearing in Brazil, through trade, even with the lever at 0; the change is capped between none and double today's. The lever then stops its share of what is left. Both are CO₂ changes entered in the climate response like any other.

**Carbon in farmland** (see [Carbon stored in farmland](#method-removals)). Cover crops, trees on farms, biochar and rock dust store CO₂: adoption × the most of the land it can reach (`maxShare`) × the hectares of cropland, pasture or both once the shift is complete × the country's rate, for `years` years from when each hectare adopts it. Cover crops and trees saturate after 20 years; biochar and rock dust keep storing to 2100.

**Energy and transport** (see [Energy and transport](#method-sectors)). Seven levers on the fossil CO₂ of the scenario chosen under Options: electric cars and electric trucks (their tailpipe CO₂ less the electricity they draw on the grid), less flying (aviation's CO₂ and its non-CO₂ forcing), clean aviation fuel (aviation's CO₂ only), clean shipping, renewable electricity (the electricity sector's CO₂, and with it the grid's intensity that electric vehicles and data centres draw on) and data centres on clean power (the extra demand of AI data centres, when Options count it). Their CO₂ change is turned into temperature with the CO₂ curve, and the change in aviation's non-CO₂ forcing with the temperature response to forcing. The fuel they cut takes with it the methane leaked producing it and the air pollution burning it made, so they act on air pollution too: less cooling masked, a cost in the first decades that the methane saved about offsets.

Natural regrowth on freed farmland also takes up carbon, beside the four practices of carbon in farmland, but it is an option (*Freed farmland*, under Options), not a lever group (see [Land](#method-land)). It acts on land the shift and less waste free; avoided clearing is CO₂ that is not emitted on forest that stays, not land, so the two never count the same hectare.

## How levers combine

- Practices on the same source multiply rather than add: two practices that each remove 30% and 20% of enteric methane remove 1 − 0.7 × 0.8 = 44%, not 50%.
- Less waste acts on the diet after the shift, practices on the production left after both, the deforestation lever on the clearing left after the shift and less waste. Each lever therefore does a little less when combined than alone; the breakdown below shares that overlap out evenly.
- The levers' full effect ramps in linearly with the shift. Where a lever acts on the change in production (practices on the herd and crops the shift and less waste changed, the deforestation lever on the clearing they changed), that part ramps in as both do: with a share r of the way reached, the lever's r × cut acts on r × the change, so it grows as r².
- Removals act on the farmland left once the shift is complete; a shift that frees cropland leaves less for cover crops. They ramp in linearly: mid-ramp they count land the shift has not yet freed or used.
- Energy levers combine with each other, not with the farming levers: renewable electricity makes electric vehicles and data centres save more; less flying and clean fuel each act on the aviation CO₂ the other leaves, so both at 50% cut 75% of it.

## Reading the breakdown

The summary shows the warming avoided at the chosen year, under each cause the levers act on, biggest first: for each cause, what the levers avoid of it, then the part of each lever group. A group acting on two causes appears under both, with its part of each. The parts add up exactly to the total, and the causes to the reductions the Causes of warming chart shows.

Levers overlap: less waste on a diet that has already shifted saves less than on today's diet, practices cut less on a smaller herd, and regrowth needs the land a shift or less waste frees. Counting the groups in turn, one first and the next on what is left, would give the overlap to whichever group comes later, and so make the order a choice of which lever looks bigger. Planetshift uses the **Shapley value** instead: a group's part is the mean, over every order in which the acting groups could be counted, of what it adds to the ones counted before it. The parts add up to the total, a group that adds nothing gets nothing, two groups that act alike get the same, and listing the groups in another order changes nothing. A lever that only acts with another, such as regrowth on freed land, shares that joint effect half and half with it.

Computed directly, the Shapley value needs one run of the model for every set of acting groups, 2ⁿ runs (256 with all eight groups acting), about 2.8 s on the real data; far beyond the budget of a slider move. Planetshift uses how the groups interact instead. Farming's groups interact only through the production they change: the diet and less waste change what is produced, and practices, deforestation, regrowth and carbon in farmland act on the production those two leave, but not on each other. So the worth of any set of farming groups is read from one pass of the farm emissions per set of production groups in it (none, the diet, less waste, both): at most four passes, three beyond the run itself, each kept while the sliders that do not change it move. The groups outside farming (energy and transport, buildings and industry) interact with each other, through the grid renewable electricity leaves, and not with farming: their worth is their own emissions recomputed with the other's levers off. A test checks, on the fixtures and on the real data, that this gives the Shapley values of full runs to nine digits.

A second method was considered: each group's effect alone, scaled so the parts add up to the total. It needs the same passes, so it is no faster, and it is less fair: a group whose effect depends on another gets nothing (regrowth alone frees no land, so it would vanish from the breakdown, its uptake given to the diet and less waste), and the overlap is shared in proportion to size rather than evenly. On the real data with every group acting, the two differ by up to a quarter of a group's part.

Measured on the real data, a run takes a median of 5 ms over the test samples (4.5 ms before), and 17 ms with every lever and every option on as the food-waste slider moves (10 ms before), within the 20 and 30 ms budgets.

With permafrost on, the change in its release is shared out between the groups, and between the causes, in proportion to the size of each one's avoided warming, whatever its sign, so the parts still add up. Shared by the signed values, a part that adds warming would take a negative share; but the energy levers avoid warming under fossil fuels and add some under air pollution, and where the two nearly cancel (until about 2040) each part would take many times the whole change. By size, no part moves by more than the change itself. The avoided emissions in CO₂e in the year the levers are complete are shared out the same way; they leave out aviation's non-CO₂ forcing and permafrost, which are not emissions of a lever.

## Why some levers are uncertain

The ranges of the practices (a low and high reduction) and of the removals (a low and high rate) feed the likely range of the warming avoided, with the climate response: the low end takes the smaller reduction and the slower uptake. For the energy levers, the low end takes the most electricity per kilogram of tailpipe CO₂ avoided, the least non-CO₂ forcing per kilogram of aviation CO₂ and the lowest data-centre demand. Some uncertainty is not in the range. Whether a practice can be adopted in a country (`applicable`) is a judgement. Soil carbon is hard to measure and can be lost again. Deforestation's tie to production is a reading of consumption-based footprints, not a law: clearing follows roads, prices and enforcement as much as demand. Wasting less frees money that is spent on something else, a rebound the model leaves out. And the levers are adoption shares, not policies: the model says what they would avoid, not what it would take to reach them.

## Assumptions

- Every lever, the diet included, ramps in linearly from the current year to the year *All levers fully in place by*, and holds after.
- Farm-practice reductions apply to the emissions of the production with the shift and the other levers; the baseline keeps today's practices.
- A practice's reduction on the same source combines multiplicatively with the others'; its range is read low for the low end of the warming avoided.
- An item without a split by source takes the world's, then the other scope's; without any, practices do not act on it.
- Deforestation CO₂ is a constant stream, not scaled with population; it moves with each item's relative production change in the country (between −100% and +100%), and the lever stops its share of the rest.
- Removals act on the land once the shift is complete and store for `years` years from each hectare's adoption; the rate is the country's, else the world's.
- The breakdown is each group's Shapley value over the acting groups, split by cause; the permafrost change is shared out in proportion to the size of each part.
- Energy levers cut a share of each sector's CO₂ in the scenario chosen under Options, ramping in like the shift; they do not change farm emissions, and electricity used on farms keeps its own intensity.
- A missing lever file leaves its levers without effect, shown as not available.
